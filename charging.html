Research / Project

Active doctoral research

Triboelectric charging & process physics

I study how particle–surface contact and operating conditions influence the electrical charge acquired by biomass components.

Research question

How do contact material, tube length and particle dosing affect net charge and subsequent separation?

Approach

I use a fluidized feed system, contact tubes and a Faraday cup/electrometer to examine model biomass components. Materials investigated include cellulose, lignin, xylan and protein, with PVC, nylon, PTFE and copper contact surfaces.

Preliminary interpretation

Measurements on cellulose and lignin indicate that charge polarity and magnitude can depend on contact material and dosing. Net charge alone cannot resolve the distribution of individual particle charges.

Next steps

Measure repeatability and charge distributions where feasible, control moisture and particle size, and connect charge measurements to fraction composition.
